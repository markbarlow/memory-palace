The new CEO flew in from the US and showed up at the office in a black turtle neck jumper which screamed "cargo cult victim". Appearances were not deceiving.

The first cull was face-to-face, chopping most of the customer care team and halving other departments to leave some twenty people. A year later, with no progress on his celebrity-fawning pivot, he literally phoned it in from Santa Monica. In London we huddled in the small office on [Brune Street](https://maps.app.goo.gl/NCfqWnRiWs7L6ve88) near Spitalfields market and were presented with a handful of slides accompanied by mealy-mouthed words about headwinds. At the end we looked around quizzically: did that mean what we thought it meant?  

After some concerned conversations we went back to our desks to carry on work. Mel, the HR assistant, stood up in the corner with a pile of white envelopes in hand. She started moving along the rows and handing them out, like the Grim Reaper. The cowardly CEO had switched from personal visits to letters, in an echo of José Saramago's [Death at Intervals](https://www.goodreads.com/book/show/6238698), with Mel as his put-apon courier. Like the book this had a farcical element to it, but unlike the book there was a complete absence of humour. Eyes and anger were concentrated on Mel and her creeping death. 

Jeff had popped out to the toilet and missed this development entirely, so Mel hesitated when she got to his desk. Undeterred, she popped the letter down for him to discover on his return. He would be one of the people most upset late into the evening. My delivery was up next, the letter handed over. I ripped the seal of the envelope and pulled the predictable contents out of its throat. 

We headed en masse to [The Magpie](https://maps.app.goo.gl/xEkdDkhvbunYW4mDA), where the sliver lining was a late afternoon and evening full of camaraderie, satire and amusing tales of the pitfalls of online community management. Gallow's humour obliged us all to try a pint of Proper Job, which set the tone perfectly.
