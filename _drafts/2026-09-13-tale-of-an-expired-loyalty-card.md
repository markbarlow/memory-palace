The loyalty card is pale white, enlivened with a bright flower logo, and embossed with her name. It sits atop sundry litter in a black bin on the pavement in the retail park, outside the eateries and bowling centre before the main strip of shops.

Ten minutes earlier we were up the road at the [Dobbies garden centre](https://www.dobbies.com/liverpool) making one last use of her card before the cancelled subscription expired. On the one hand it felt a bit like stealing her drink while she's asleep. On the other, it fell into that permissive category of "It's what she would have wanted". She hated waste and knew I loved coffee. We used to come here a lot all together too, especially when the boys were younger. With the outside space, a children's play area plus a pet shop with an aquarium there was plenty to entertain them. The last of these is still a draw.

The coffee tastes of absence and melancholia.

Now we've come to the retail park nearby, a site tentatively emerging from the deep sleep of the third lockdown. As we walk from the car I am thumbing the card and turning it over in my pocket. I take a deep breath and acknowledge that this needs to be done. Privately and discretely I slip the card into the bin as we walk past, bid it goodbye, and only just manage to hold back a welling of tears.
